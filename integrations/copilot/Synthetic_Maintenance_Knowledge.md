# Demo maintenance knowledge base

All content below is fictional demonstration material for a portfolio prototype. It is not an operational procedure and must not be used to operate real equipment.

## ARTICLE: SOP-PUMP-VIBRATION
Title: Pump vibration inspection - initial analyst checklist
Asset types: centrifugal pump, motor, bearing
Keywords: vibration, bearing, pump, noise, shaft, misalignment
Procedure:
1. Review the trend and compare it with the site's approved alarm threshold.
2. Check the asset history and recent maintenance work orders.
3. Ask the operator to confirm whether the vibration is new, increasing, or accompanied by noise, heat, or leakage.
4. If the approved site alarm is exceeded or conditions appear unsafe, stop troubleshooting and follow the local safety and escalation procedure.
5. A qualified maintenance person decides whether inspection or isolation is required.
Limitations: This checklist does not define safe operating limits or authorise physical intervention.

## ARTICLE: SOP-MOTOR-OVERHEAT
Title: Motor overheating - triage guidance
Asset types: motor, pump, fan, conveyor
Keywords: motor, heat, hot, overheating, temperature, smell
Procedure:
1. Record the reported temperature, timestamp, asset ID, and operating context if available.
2. Compare the reading with the equipment manufacturer's approved limits and the site's alarm policy.
3. Check whether the issue coincides with load changes, blocked ventilation, or a recent maintenance event.
4. If there is smoke, burning smell, visible damage, or immediate danger, use the site's emergency procedure and alert the authorised response team.
5. Do not restart equipment solely on the basis of an AI-generated recommendation.
Limitations: Do not use this article as a substitute for manufacturer documentation or site safety rules.

## ARTICLE: SOP-LEAK-DETECTION
Title: Suspected leak - information gathering and escalation
Asset types: pump, valve, pipe, hydraulic system
Keywords: leak, leakage, fluid, pressure, spill, seal
Procedure:
1. Record the suspected substance if known, location, asset ID, and whether the leak is increasing.
2. If the substance is unknown, hazardous, or creating an immediate risk, avoid contact and follow the local emergency and isolation procedure.
3. Notify the authorised site response or maintenance lead.
4. Review the approved asset diagram and maintenance history only if authorised.
5. Do not instruct unqualified personnel to tighten, dismantle, or isolate equipment.
Limitations: This fictional article intentionally omits specific isolation steps and operational thresholds.

## ARTICLE: SOP-CONVEYOR-JAM
Title: Conveyor jam - safe triage information
Asset types: conveyor, production line, motor
Keywords: conveyor, jam, belt, blockage, production line, stuck
Procedure:
1. Capture the conveyor ID, affected line, observed symptoms, and production impact.
2. Check the relevant approved work instruction and recent fault history.
3. Route the incident to the designated maintenance team and record any production dependency.
4. Do not ask an operator to reach into, bypass, or restart jammed machinery based on a chat response.
5. Restart or maintenance activity requires the site's authorised procedure and qualified personnel.
Limitations: This article is not a lockout/tagout procedure.

## ARTICLE: SOP-SENSOR-QUALITY
Title: Sensor reading quality checks
Asset types: temperature sensor, vibration sensor, pressure sensor
Keywords: sensor, telemetry, missing data, drift, spike, inconsistent reading
Procedure:
1. Check timestamp, units, asset mapping, and whether values are missing or duplicated.
2. Compare against a recent known-good period and, where available, a redundant measurement.
3. Distinguish equipment symptoms from likely data-quality issues; do not assume a single abnormal reading proves asset failure.
4. Record the source and confidence of the measurement.
5. Escalate unexplained readings for qualified review before any operational decision.
Limitations: Any data threshold must be supplied by approved site or manufacturer documentation.

## ARTICLE: SOP-INCIDENT-PRIORITY
Title: Incident priority - demonstration routing rules
Asset types: all
Keywords: priority, urgency, safety, downtime, production impact, severity
Procedure:
1. A credible safety concern is immediately routed for human safety review.
2. High production impact or extended downtime may increase operational priority.
3. Missing information should be requested rather than guessed.
4. The assigned site's approved incident policy overrides this demonstration rubric.
5. AI may suggest a category and priority; a designated human owns the final decision.
Limitations: These are demo routing principles, not a certified industrial incident management standard.
